	\begin{abstract}
	The rapid integration of multimodal Large Language Models (LLMs) into smart agriculture has demonstrated significant potential, yet existing conversational assistants often yield generic responses that lack actionable, localized precision. While context-amplified architectures leveraging multi-domain Earth observation data address this limitation by generating parcel-specific insights, evaluating their non-deterministic outputs remains a critical open challenge. Current evaluation frameworks lack a method to assess how dynamically retrieved context is utilized, nor can they quantify the specific impact of individual architectural modules. To bridge this gap, this paper proposes a novel, reproducible evaluation methodology specifically tailored for context-amplified multimodal LLMs. We introduce a hybrid, human-in-the-loop "Mixture-of-Experts" panel—comprising two human evaluators and two distinct LLM judges—to assess a comprehensive dataset of 143 queries across five targeted metrics. The core analytical novelty of this framework lies in the design of an ablation study modeled as a cooperative game, utilizing Shapley values to mathematically quantify the marginal contributions of distinct data modules (Satellite, Orthophoto, and Retrieval-Augmented Generation). Our results demonstrate that visual modules, acting as the system's "eyes" and are indispensable for spatial reasoning, driving up to a 32$\%$ increase in overall response utility. Conversely, the RAG module exhibits high, query-dependent volatility. Additional analyses on context length and inter-evaluator consensus confirm the system's resilience to information overload and highlight the necessity of human oversight in penalizing hallucinations. Accompanied by a fully open-source dataset, this methodology establishes a rigorous, methodical standard for benchmarking the reasoning and contextual grounding of next-generation foundation models in agriculture.
	\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{introduction}

Geospatial foundation models like Cambridge's Tessera, are trained on satellite images to support a wide range of environmental monitoring tasks. However, most are too difficult to use or understand by their intended users, according to \cite{young_how_2026}.

\noindent\rule{\columnwidth}{0.4pt}

The rapid development of multimodal Large Language Models (LLMs) has unlocked a huge span of unprecedented applications across diverse scientific and industrial domains. Among these, smart agriculture and precision farming stand out as areas of great potential. The recent emergence of geofoundation models (\cite{geofoundation_2023, geofoundation_2026}) and Earth embeddings (\cite{earth_embeddings}), coupled with the swift evolution of generative language models, holds immense promise for Earth observation and agricultural management. This progress has been significantly accelerated by open-source initiatives, which have democratized access to foundational models and driven community-led breakthroughs in applied Artificial Intelligence (AI).

In recent years, a growing body of literature has explored the integration of generative AI into agricultural workflows. Many of these initiatives have materialized as conversational assistants tailored for Earth observation and agronomy (\cite{yuan_omnigeo_2025, kuckreja_geochat_2023}). These studies frequently leverage Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), geo-embeddings, or hybrid approaches combining language modeling with classical computer vision tasks, such as the semantic segmentation of satellite imagery and orthophotos. However, despite these technical advances, existing approaches remain largely fragmented. While the outputs generated by these state-of-the-art tools are increasingly accurate and explore new frontiers in data accessibility, they fundamentally remain generic, failing to provide the highly localized, actionable insights required for precise, parcel-level terrain management.

To bridge this gap, we recently proposed a novel multimodal LLM framework capable of generating highly specific, localized responses regarding designated land regions for agricultural applications (\cite{juan_2025}). This system achieves its specificity through the retrieval, processing, and fusion of diverse, multi-domain open data sources, effectively functioning as a context-amplified tool for land management.

Following the technical validation of this architecture, a critical challenge arises: the robust and methodical evaluation of its generated outputs. Since the inception of generative AI, evaluation has remained a primary and open line of research, inherent to any application relying on non-deterministic inference. Unlike traditional machine learning paradigms, where evaluation metrics rely on a finite set of ground-truth labels, the space of correct responses in generative modeling is virtually infinite—a single question can be correctly answered in multiple, equally valid ways depending on the reasoning approach. Numerous evaluation frameworks have been proposed to navigate this complexity. For instance, the LLM-as-a-judge paradigm (\cite{zheng_judging_2023})—which we initially employed in our previous work—is widely adopted due to its simplicity and low operational cost. Nevertheless, this technique is highly vulnerable to technical hallucinations, suffers from self-reference bias, and tends to lose consistency over complex reasoning chains. Similarly, frameworks like RAGAS (\cite{es_ragas_2025}) have been developed specifically to evaluate RAG pipelines, and substantial research has been devoted to optimizing automated evaluation metrics. Despite this rich landscape of evaluation tools, none of the existing frameworks align with the complex requirements of our context-amplified architecture. Specifically, evaluating this system demands more than merely assessing the factual correctness of the final output; it requires interrogating how the model utilized the available external context. A rigorous evaluation must distinguish between a response that is "good but generic" and one that is "specific and context-aware." Furthermore, it must discern whether a high-quality response stems merely from the model's robust foundational pre-training, or if it is the result of successful reasoning over the dynamically retrieved multi-domain data.

To address these unmet needs, this paper proposes a novel, reproducible evaluation methodology tailored for context-amplified multimodal LLMs in agricultural data applications. Our approach introduces a multi-stage, human-in-the-loop (hybrid human-machine) framework designed to mitigate automated biases. The most challenging and innovative aspect of this framework lies in quantifying the marginal contributions of individual architectural modules. The methodology allows to understand not only the final quality of a generated response, but exactly how and to what extent each specific module influenced that quality. To achieve this, we design a comprehensive ablation study based on a factorial design, systematically evaluating different configurations by isolating, adding, and removing system modules. The evaluation results are subsequently analyzed through collaborative game theory, providing a rigorous mathematical framework to measure the precise impact and value added by each component. The entirely of this methodology and its associated tools are open source. Furthermore, to ensure complete reproducibility and to provide a valuable asset for the research community, we have published our complete evaluation dataset on Zenodo as supplementary material. This open dataset comprises 143 detailed evaluations, containing the specific agricultural parcels, the exact external context retrieved, and the corresponding responses generated by the model. \textcolor{red}{¿esta ultima parte igual no queda bien en la intro?}

The remainder of this article is structured as follows: Section 2 details the architecture of the conversational system, provides a functional overview, and extensively outlines the evaluation methodology, including parcel selection, human-in-the-loop judge guidelines, evaluation stages, utilized metrics, and the design of the ablation study. Section 3 presents and discusses the results derived from the evaluation and the game theory analysis. Finally, Section 4 summarizes the main conclusions drawn from this work.

\subsection{System Architecture and Overview}
The system to be evaluated integrates heterogeneous geospatial, remote sensing, and agronomic domain knowledge into a unified data processing pipeline to ground the reasoning of a LLM. As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}, the architecture is structured into two primary layers: the \textit{User Interface} and the \textit{Backend Processing} pipeline. The core objective is to enrich the user's inquiry regarding a specific agricultural plot with multi-modal and biophysical context. The execution pipeline proceeds through five coordinated stages: (I) Plot Info Retrieval: The user provides a target plot identifier along with a natural language query. The system queries the geographic information system registry (SIGPAC) to retrieve plot-level attributes—including slope, elevation, surface area, perimeter, and registered land use—along with its georeferenced boundary geometry (encoded as GeoJSON). Hereafter, this piece of context is regarded as Plot Data. (II) Context Retrieval: Exploiting the extracted plot geometry, this module orchestrates two parallel multi-modal processing workflows: (II-a) Visual terrain analysis: High-resolution aerial imagery (orthophoto) covering the delineated plot is retrieved and processed by a Multimodal LLM (MLLM) conditioned on the geometric and cadastral metadata, yielding a structured natural-language description of terrain topology, visible infrastructure, and surface patterns. (II-b) Biophysical state extraction: Multi-spectral imagery from the Sentinel-2 constellation (Copernicus, European Space Agency) is acquired. Following spatio-temporal filtering and band synthesis over the plot geometry, the pipeline computes a set of spectral vegetation and water indices, including NDVI, EVI and NDWI. (III) Knowledge Retrieval: In parallel with context generation, a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) subsystem queries a document repository created by the user. (IV) Data Fusion and Context Aggregation: The diverse data modalities—cadastral metadata, MLLM-derived terrain descriptions, remote sensing spectral indices, and retrieved textual context—are aligned and harmonized within an aggregator. This module serializes multi-source representations into a structured natural-language prompt context. (V) LLM Reasoning and Answer Generation: The synthesized prompt is fed to the central reasoning LLM, which integrates the holistic parcel profile to formulate the final recommendations delivered back to the user. For further details on the inference framework and technical hardware and software components, see \cite{juan_2025}. 

\subsection{Evaluation Framework}

The architectural characteristics of the proposed system dictate the requirements for its evaluation methodology. Crucially, this framework is not conceived merely as an \textit{ad-hoc} assessment for this specific tool, but rather as a reproducible and adaptable evaluation standard for any modular conversational agent relying on augmented external context. The primary challenge distinguishing this methodology from standard LLM benchmarks is the necessity to evaluate the actual utilization of geospatial context during response generation, going beyond mere response quality. An output that is factually correct but generic, or generated solely from the model's pre-trained memory, cannot be considered equivalent to a personalized answer grounded in the specific biophysical aspects of a given terrain. Consequently, a comprehensive methodology combining multiple experimental phases, modular configurations, and tailored metrics has been designed.

\subsubsection{Two-Stage Experimental Design}
The evaluation pipeline is structured into two main sequential phases to optimize operational resources while maintaining statistical robustness: an overall performance assessment and an ablation study. The first phase, named \textit{Broad Performance Assessment}, evaluates the system's holistic capability across diverse scenarios, using the full architecture. This stage does not represent a novel contribution, as it is similar to existing evaluation methods; rather, it provides an overall view of the quality of the responses in general terms, which is necessary before moving on to a more specific evaluation of the architecture modules. Table \ref{tab:num_queries_parcels} contians the number of evaluation queries, resulting in roughly 50$\%$ for each stage.

The second phase consists of an \textit{Ablation Study} intended to quantify the marginal contribution of each architectural component (Orthophoto, Satellite, and RAG modules, previously introduced in Fig. \ref{fig:architecture}). The concept of ablation study, originally rooted in medicine and biology to describe the surgical removal of tissue to determine its physiological function, has been adapted to the field of AI and proved as a valid paradigm to uncover individual contributions by isolating components. In \cite{meyes_ablation_2020}, they use the concept of ablation for finding redundancies in the representations of Artifitial Neural Networks. In this work, we extend this analytical framework to evaluate our proposed modular methodology by progressively isolating and bypassing individual modules. For this porpuse, a full factorial design of experiments was executed to set up the configurations, which are combinations of the modules, summarized in Fig. \ref{fig:ablation_modules}\reviewjulio{No entiendo esta figura.}. By systematically enabling and disabling these three external context modules, $2^3 = 8$ configurations were generated, plus a Zero-Shot baseline, where the LLM reasons without any external parcel context, for a total of 9 configurations, as detailed in Table \ref{tab:ablation_configs}. For this phase, a representative subset of one parcel per LU category was selected, and 2 specific queries were executed across all 9 configurations, yielding 72 additional evaluations (Tab. \ref{tab:num_queries_parcels}).

\subsubsection{Panel of judges}

A simplified approach to response evaluation, as utilized in our \reviewjulio{Creo que es necesario añadir nuevamente la referencia.}, relies exclusively on the LLM-as-a-judge framework. This paradigm offers distinct advantages in terms of execution speed and scalability, allowing for the rapid assessment of large datasets without the bottlenecks associated with human review. Conversely, assembling a panel of human evaluators requires substantial planning, timeframes, and financial resources. However, human evaluation remains indispensable, as it fundamentally reduces the leniency bias frequently exhibited by LLM evaluators (\cite{gu_survey_2026}) and provides a significantly higher degree of reliability when assessing nuanced, domain-specific reasoning. To effectively navigate this trade-off between scalability and qualitative rigor, we designed a hybrid, human-in-the-loop evaluation strategy. By merging human and machine critical judgement, this methodology not only optimizes resource allocation but also systematically mitigates the mono-dimensional biases inherent to using either approach in isolation. 

Consequently, the definitive panel of judges comprises four distinct entities: two human evaluators and two LLM-based evaluators. To ensure both technical depth and practical accessibility, the human contingent is divided into two distinct roles, a domain expert (holding a PhD in Biology), regarded hereafter as "Human Expert" (H$\_$E) that provides rigorous scientific scrutiny; and a control evaluator, regarded as "Human Control" (H$\_$C), representing the end-user perspective in terms of clarity and usability for non-academic audience. For the LLM side, we integrated two diverse language models with comparable computational footprints into the panel: a propietary model (LLM$\_$G), \textit{Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite}, and an open-source model (LLM$\_$Q), \textit{Qwen3.5-397B-A17B-Instruct}.

\subsection{Multidimensional metrics}

The quality of the generated responses is quantified using a multidimensional 5-point Likert scale encompassing five distinct criteria (see Table \ref{tab:evaluation_metrics}). Four of these metrics—\textit{Correctness, Completeness, Relevance,} and \textit{Utility}—evaluate the intrinsic quality of the response and can be assessed without access to the retrieved context. Conversely, the \textit{Grounding} metric strictly requires contextual reference; it is specifically designed to measure the degree to which the formulated response is faithfully derived from the injected multi-domain data rather than the model's pre-existing generalized knowledge. In doing so, it serves as a critical diagnostic tool to identify and penalize \textit{internal knowledge over-reliance}, a phenomenon where an LLM disregards or underutilizes dynamically retrieved evidence, defaulting instead to its internalized knowledge. This distinction carries profound practical implications for model selection and hardware feasibility in precision agriculture. The computational overhead of an LLM is directly related to the depth and scope of its internal knowledge base. Consequently, executing large foundation models locally on edge devices remains computationally prohibitive. If empirical evaluation reveals that domain-specific accuracy is primarily governed by robust contextual grounding rather than massive internal knowledge, high-performance agricultural reasoning can be achieved using significantly smaller, quantized models.

\subsection{Mathematical analysis of the results}

This section describes the mathematical quantification of the ablation study results. Evaluating queries across diverse architectural configurations yields empirical data on marginal contributions; however, there is yet a need to quantify the intrinsic value added by each of the three modules to the final response quality. To provide a rigorous quantification, we model the multi-modular architecture within the mathematical framework of cooperative game theory. In this paradigm, the system's structural modules act as players collaborating to produce a unified outcome, the system's response. By applying Shapley values (\cite{shapley1953value}), a foundational concept from cooperative game theory that has been widely adapted for feature attribution in explainable AI with the SHAP values (\cite{lundberg_unified_2017}), we can fairly and precisely distribute the total system performance among its constituent components based on their true marginal contributions across all possible combinations. Formally, let $N$ be the set of all players. In our architecture, the players correspond to the three primary modules: $N = \{M_{S}, M_{O}, M_{R}\}$. Let $S$ denote any subset, or \textit{coalition}, of these modules. The general Shapley value $\phi_i(v)$ for a module $i$ given a performance value function $v$ is computed as:

\begin{equation}
	\phi_i(v) = \sum_{S \subseteq N \setminus \{i\}} \frac{|S|!(|N| - |S| - 1)!}{|N|!} \big(v(S \cup \{i\}) - v(S)\big)
\end{equation}

By instantiating this general formula for our specific three-player game, we isolate the exact contribution of each module across all possible permutations:

\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        \phi_S(v) = {} & \frac{1}{3} \big[ v(\{M_S\}) - v(\varnothing) \big]              \\
                    & + \frac{1}{6} \big[ v(\{M_S, M_O\}) - v(\{M_O\}) \big]           \\
                    & + \frac{1}{6} \big[ v(\{M_S, M_R\}) - v(\{M_R\}) \big]           \\
                    & + \frac{1}{3} \big[ v(\{M_S, M_O, M_R\}) - v(\{M_O, M_R\}) \big]
    \end{split}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        \phi_O(v) = {} & \frac{1}{3}[v(\{M_O\})-v(\varnothing)]           \\
                    & +\frac{1}{6}[v(\{M_S, M_O\})-v(\{M_S\})]         \\
                    & +\frac{1}{6}[v(\{M_O, M_R\})-v(\{M_R\})]         \\
                    & +\frac{1}{3}[v(\{M_S, M_O,M_R\})-v(\{M_S,M_R\})]
    \end{split}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        \phi_R(v) = {} & \frac{1}{3}[v(\{M_R\})-v(\varnothing)]           \\
                    & +\frac{1}{6}[v(\{M_S, M_R\})-v(\{M_S\})]         \\
                    & +\frac{1}{6}[v(\{M_O, M_R\})-v(\{M_O\})]         \\
                    & +\frac{1}{3}[v(\{M_S, M_O,M_R\})-v(\{M_S,M_O\})]
    \end{split}
\end{equation}

By substituting the conceptual coalitions with the empirical performance scores obtained from their corresponding configurations (Table \ref{tab:ablation_configs}), we rewrite the equation in terms of configuration performances:

\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        \phi_S(v) = {} & \frac{1}{3}[v(C_1)-v(C_0)]+\frac{1}{6}[v(C_3)-v(C_2)]  \\
                    & +\frac{1}{6}[v(C_5)-v(C_4)]+\frac{1}{3}[v(C_7)-v(C_6)]
    \end{split}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        \phi_O(v) = {} & \frac{1}{3}[v(C_2)-v(C_0)]+\frac{1}{6}[v(C_3)-v(C_1)]  \\
                    & +\frac{1}{6}[v(C_6)-v(C_4)]+\frac{1}{3}[v(C_7)-v(C_5)]
    \end{split}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        \phi_R(v) = {} & \frac{1}{3}[v(C_4)-v(C_0)]+\frac{1}{6}[v(C_5)-v(C_1)]  \\
                    & +\frac{1}{6}[v(C_6)-v(C_2)]+\frac{1}{3}[v(C_7)-v(C_3)]
    \end{split}
\end{equation}

Through this formulation, the outputs of the ablation configurations are transformed into distinct, quantified metrics that represent the definitive analytical weight of each architectural module. Furthermore, we exploit the inherent mathematical property of additive efficiency of Shapley values by formally defining the \textit{Global Delta} as the sum of the three individual modular contributions. This metric encapsulates the total aggregate gain in the system's performance. In practical terms, it quantifies the exact quality uplift achieved strictly through the integration of the external data sources, serving as measure to compare against conventional language models.

\subsection{Marginal contributions}

The true analytical depth of this methodology emerges when examining the ablation study. Fig. \ref{fig:results} illustrates the performance variations across the different architectural configurations, grouped by the active status of the individual modules (Satellite, Orthophoto, and RAG). A preliminary visual inspection of these configurations immediately suggests that certain modules possess a more dominant influence on the final response quality than others. To rigorously quantify these visual trends and map the exact marginal contribution of each module, we analyze the calculated Shapley values, computed as presented in the previous section. As depicted in the boxplots of Fig. \ref{fig:shapleys}, the distribution of these values reveals distinct behavioral profiles for the three modules. In this framework, values to the right of zero indicate a positive contribution to the response quality, zero implies a neutral impact, and negative values denote a detrimental effect, meaning the inclusion of the module actively degraded the output.The Orthophoto module ($M_O$) emerges as the most critical contributor to the system's success. As shown in the distributions, its inclusion can add up to 2 to 3 points (on a 5-point scale) to the Completeness and Utility metrics. This can be explained due to the fact that this module is responsible for giving "eyes" to the system; hence asking the LLM to resolve a query without it is akin to asking it to analyze a parcel blindfolded. The Satellite module ($M_S$) also consistently yields positive Shapley values, contributing reliably, although more moderately, to the outputs. In  contrast, the RAG module ($M_R$) exhibits the most complex and nuanced behavior. While it provides positive contributions in specific scenarios, it frequently registers negative Shapley values, indicating that its activation penalized the response quality. This phenomenon is consistent with the fundamental mechanics of Retrieval-Augmented Generation, as RAG is heavily dependent on the availability of relevant external documentation and the semantic similarity between the user's query and the knowledge base. Therefore, when a query targets a topic with scarce or irrelevant documentation, the retrieval pipeline injects noise into the prompt context, confusing the LLM and degrading its output. Furthermore, unlike the visual modules (which are deterministically invoked whenever enabled), the RAG module's operation is non-deterministic; its actual utilization relies on the LLM's internal triage capabilities and the effectiveness of the semantic rerankers, making it more volatile. The numerical values of these insights is fully detailed in Table \ref{tab:shapley_extensive} (Appendix A), which catalogues the Shapley values and introduces the previously defined \textit{Global Delta} ($\Delta_{global}$). By analyzing this aggregate metric, we can definitively quantify the overarching value of the context-amplified architecture. For instance, the data demonstrates that, on average, the generated responses are 1.625 points out of 5 (a direct 32$\%$) more useful strictly as a result of integrating the external, multi-domain context compared to relying solely on the foundational model's knowledge.

Having established the overarching performance trends and the aggregate marginal contributions of each module, we now discuss the ablation results within query-level analysis. A fundamental mathematical property of Shapley values is their linearity, which allows for the decomposition of the global game into individual experiments (queries), making it possible to identify which types of questions draw the most value from each architectural component. Fig. \ref{fig:shapleys_queries_2} illustrates the computed Shapley values for the 8 distinct queries of the ablation study, aggregated averaging the scores across all four judges and the five evaluation metrics for the sake of clarity. The resulting heatmap provides empirical evidence of how the semantic nature of a query dictates module utilization. For instance, the Satellite module ($M_S$) registers its highest marginal contribution (0.725) in response to the query: "Describe the density of this forest." By injecting satellite-derived vegetation indices, this module provides the system with the knowledge to provide a much more accurate response to this given question. Even more striking is the behavior of the Orthophoto module ($M_O$), which dominates the marginal contributions for spatial and descriptive tasks. This is most evident in the two bottom queries: "Describe the parcel" (yielding a Shapley value of 1.558) and "What kind of activities does this terrain allow?" (yielding 2.658). Both of these queries are heavily dependent on immediate visual context and structural terrain features. Thus, the activation of the visual module triggers a massive improvement in response quality. Conversely, the heatmap further reinforces the volatile nature of the RAG module ($M_R$) reiterating the strong dependence on the contribution of this module with the nature of the question and the provided documents. Ultimately, the observed volatility and lower average contribution of the RAG module should not be interpreted as an architectural flaw, but rather as an indicator of the system's operational flexibility. Because its efficacy is fundamentally related to the specific use case and the quality of the user's provided documentation, the module's true value is to be maximized by the practitioners themselves.  

\subsection{Inter-Evaluator Reliability and Judge Consensus}

\begin{figure*}[h!]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{PIC/consensus.png}
	\caption{Judges agreement computed using the Kendall's $\tau$. * means p$<$0.05, ** means p$<$0.01, *** means p$<$0.001.}
	\label{fig:consensus}
\end{figure*}

Understanding the degree of alignment among our hybrid panel of judges is crucial for validating the robustness of the proposed methodology. Fig. \ref{fig:consensus} visualizes the inter-evaluator agreement across all five metrics. To quantify this consensus, we employed Kendall’s rank correlation coefficient ($\tau$). Kendall's $\tau$ is more robust than Spearman's correlation for this specific dataset, as it mathematically accounts for the high frequency of tied ranks inherently produced by discrete 5-point Likert scales. Overall, the matrices reveal a solid baseline of agreement, validating the general coherence of the hybrid Mixture of Experts panel. The correlations exhibit expected patterns in both intra-group consensus (alignment between the two human judges and between the two LLMs) and inter-group consensus (human versus machine criteria), noticeable for example in the Completeness metric for the human side, or in Correctness and Grounding for the machines. As an example of discrepancy, the Human Expert ($H_E$) and the proprietary LLM judge ($LLM_G$) present a precisely $\tau = 0.00$ in Correctness. Statistically, this indicates that the occurrences of concordant and discordant rank pairs between these two judges are perfectly balanced. The root of this divergence lies in the subjective interpretation of the evaluation rubric (Appendix B), which defines Correctness as "the accuracy and truthfulness" of the response. This discrepancy is vividly illustrated in the Zero-Shot (ZS) configurations (previously observed in Fig. \ref{fig:results}). As documented in the supplementary dataset, when no external context is available, the system frequently outputs safety-aligned responses such as: “I cannot provide a specific status update [...] To give you an accurate response, I would need [...].” The Human Expert consistently assessed these responses with a maximum score of 5, rewarding the refusal to hallucinate. Conversely, $LLM_G$ heavily penalized these responses with a minimum score of 1, interpreting the refusal to provide the requested data as a failure to answer the prompt. This example exhibits the necessity of a hybrid human-in-the-loop panel, as a way to combine both criteria. 

\subsection{Context length}

\begin{figure*}[t!]
	\centering{\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{PIC/tokens_metrics.pdf}}
	\caption{Evaluation scores against the available context length in tokens for all 4 judges plus the consensus (average).}
	\label{fig:tokens_metrics}
\end{figure*}

Finally, we examine how the volume of injected information impacts the model's generation capabilities. Because the modules $M_S$ and $M_O$ operate deterministically, context length is directly correlated with the ablation configurations: adding more modules linearly increases the token count of the prompt. Fig. \ref{fig:tokens_metrics} plots the scores assigned by the judges against the context length (in tokens) for all queries. This analysis is particularly critical for the Relevance and Grounding metrics. A common vulnerability in context-augmented LLMs is the "lost in the middle" phenomenon (\cite{liu2023lostmiddlelanguagemodels}), where providing excessive information overwhelms the model's attention mechanism, causing it to generate irrelevant or poorly reasoned text. The positive upward trends across the consensus lines for both Relevance and Grounding empirically prove that our system overcomes this hurdle. Together, these two metrics validate the “sufficient and necessary” paradigm of any desirable output: the Grounding metric ensures the response possesses sufficient context-specific data to be individualized to the parcel, while the Relevance metric ensures the response contains only the necessary information, lacking of unnecessary noise. Fig. \ref{fig:tokens_metrics} demonstrates that as the context expands, the model successfully discerns relevant information through the multi-domain data. It must be noted that the capabilities of the system regarding the context length is heavily reliant on the token-window capacities and attention mechanisms of each specific language model.

\section{Conclusions}

This work builds upon our previous research, which introduced a context-amplified multimodal Large Language Model designed to provide parcel-specific agricultural insights. Following its technical implementation, we faced the critical challenge of systematically evaluating its generative outputs. Because no existing evaluation framework aligned with the requirements our system, we designed and proposed a novel reproducible evaluation framework that can be seamlessly adapted for any modular, context-amplified system. This methodology relies on a human-in-the-loop "Mixture-of-Experts" panel across five metrics. Through a process of parcel selection and query generation, we constructed a comprehensive dataset of 143 evaluation samples, each encapsulating the user query, parcel orthophoto, retrieved multi-domain context, and the model's generated response. This entire dataset, has been published as open-access supplementary material.

The proposed methodology evaluates the system across two distinct phases. While the initial Broad stage validated the model's overall performance, the true analytical power of our approach lies in the ablation stage. By framing the modular architecture as a collaborative game and applying Shapley values, we introduced a rigorous mathematical framework to quantify the exact marginal contributions of each architectural component. This analysis yielded critical insights: the Orthophoto module acts as the "eyes" of the system, proving crucual for spatial and descriptive tasks where answering blindly degrades quality entirely; the Satellite module provides reliable agronomic value for vegetation-related queries; and the RAG module exhibits high volatility, demonstrating that its effectiveness is intimately tied to the availability of specific documentation and the semantic nature of the user's prompt. Furthermore, complementary studies on inter-evaluator reliability and context length provided valuable secondary conclusions. The consensus analysis validated the hybrid judge panel while uncovering discrepancies in the interpretation of the rubric. Concurrently, the context length analysis proved that as the token count increases with the activation of multiple modules, the system maintains its Relevance and Grounding, successfully synthesizing large volumes of multi-domain data without succumbing to the "lost in the middle" phenomenon.

Ultimately, this work successfully validates not only the architecture of our context-amplified agricultural assistant but also establishes a novel, highly valuable evaluation standard. By combining rigorous mathematical modeling with a hybrid assessment panel, this open-source methodology provides the research community with a methodical, scalable, and consistent tool for evaluating the next generation of modular, multimodal LLMs in precision agriculture and beyond.
